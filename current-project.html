What one robot sees in one child’s room.

A public archive of the documents that govern ten companion robots' data. I describe each document twice, once the way the company tells it and once from the side of the people in the room who never agreed to anything.

For each robot, I archived the document that governs its data and described it with all fifteen Dublin Core elements. I pulled subject terms from FAST so they match the terms libraries already use. Companies change their policies without notice, so every record links to a dated copy on the Wayback Machine or archive.today.

  1. 01

    She calls it her friend. They roll a ball back and forth on her bedroom floor like any other afternoon.

  2. 02

    Those eyes are cameras. It can see her, her bed, and pretty much everything else in her room.

  3. 03

    It’s listening too. It can hear her, and anyone else talking nearby.

  4. 04

    And what it sees and hears doesn’t have to stay in her room. It can be sent off to the company’s servers.

  5. 05

    She’s bonding with it. A parent clicked "I agree." So who agreed?

    Now you try. Turn each feature on or off and see who can perceive what’s happening in her room.

    3parties can perceive this room

    • The childbonds with it
    • The robotsees and hears
    • The company’s serversget what it sees and hears

    This robot isn’t any one product. It stands in for the ten robots in my archive. When its eyes are lit, the camera is on. The rings mean it’s listening, and the rising numbers mean data is leaving the room.
