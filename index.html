IN PLAIN LANGUAGE

About this portfolio

I study companion robots, the kind built to keep people company. A lot of them are sold to parents for their kids or to families caring for someone. Many of these robots also record what happens around them, and the person who grows attached to one usually isn't the person who agreed to the terms. So my question is pretty simple: what should families, caregivers, and churches know before bringing one home?

This portfolio collects my work from HT 930, Digital and Public Humanities, at United Lutheran Seminary in the fall of 2026.

Under Statements, you'll find how I use digital tools in my research, my teaching, and my public theology. Work has what I made this semester, with a short note on each piece. Reflection is where I look back on what I learned.

RESEARCH PREVIEW FROM MY ARCHIVE, WHO AGREED?

What one robot sees in one child's room.

  1. 01

    She calls it her friend. They roll a ball back and forth on her bedroom floor like any other afternoon.

  2. 02

    Those eyes are cameras. It can see her, her bed, and pretty much everything else in her room.

  3. 03

    It's listening too. It can hear her, and anyone else talking nearby.

  4. 04

    And what it sees and hears doesn't have to stay in her room. It can be sent off to the company's servers.

  5. 05

    She's bonding with it. A parent clicked "I agree." So who agreed?
